# 2004 AMC 12A Problem 12

## Problem
Let A = (0,9) and B = (0,12). [Point](https://artofproblemsolving.com/wiki/index.php/Point)s A' and B' are on the [line](https://artofproblemsolving.com/wiki/index.php/line) y = x, and $\overline{AA'}$ and $\overline{BB'}$ intersect at C = (2,8). What is the length of $\overline{A'B'}$?


$$\text {(A)} 2 \qquad \text {(B)} 2\sqrt2 \qquad \text {(C)} 3 \qquad \text {(D)} 2 + \sqrt 2\qquad \text {(E)}3\sqrt 2$$

## Solution
[Image:2004_$AMC_12A-12$.png](https://artofproblemsolving.com/wiki/index.php/Image:2004_AMC_12A-12.png)

The [equation](https://artofproblemsolving.com/wiki/index.php/equation) of $\overline{AA'}$ can be found using points A, C to be y - $9 =\left(\frac{9-8}{0-2}\right)(x - 0) \Longrightarrowy =-\frac{1}{2}$x + 9. Similarily, $\overline{BB'}$ has the equation y - $12 =\left(\frac{12-8}{0-2}\right)(x-0) \Longrightarrow$ y = -$2x + 12$. These two equations intersect the line y=x at (6,6) and (4,4). Using the [distance formula](https://artofproblemsolving.com/wiki/index.php/distance_formula) or 45-45-90 [right triangle](https://artofproblemsolving.com/wiki/index.php/right_triangle)s, the answer is $2\sqrt{2}$\ $\mathrm{(B)}.$
