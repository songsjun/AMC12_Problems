==Problem==

For what odd positive integer <imath>n</imath> is the mean of <imath>1</imath>, <imath>4</imath>, <imath>9</imath>, <imath>16</imath>, <imath>\dots</imath>, <imath>n^{2}</imath> exactly <imath>32\%</imath> greater than its median?

<imath>\textbf{(A)}\   19  \qquad 	\textbf{(B)}\   39  \qquad 	\textbf{(C)}\   49  \qquad 	\textbf{(D)}\   89  \qquad 	\textbf{(E)}\   169</imath>

==Solution 1==
Since all answer choices are odd, the median of the sequence is 
<cmath>\left(\frac{n+1}{2}\right)^2</cmath>

The mean of the sequence is
<cmath>\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n} k^2 = \frac{1}{n}\cdot\frac{n(n+1)(2n+1)}{6} = \frac{(n+1)(2n+1)}{6}</cmath>

Since the mean is <imath>32\%</imath> greater than the median, we have
<cmath>\frac{(n+1)(2n+1)}{6} = \frac{132}{100}\left(\frac{n+1}{2}\right)^2</cmath>
<cmath>200n + 100 = 198n + 198</cmath>
<cmath>n = \boxed{\textbf{(C) } 49}</cmath>

==Solution 2 (Divisibility, Answer choices)==
As established in Solution 1, we have the mean and median. Now we write the ratio of of mean to median:
<cmath>\frac{\text{Mean}}{\text{Median}} = \frac{\frac{(n+1)(2n+1)}{6}}{\frac{(n+1)^2}{4}} = \frac{2(2n+1)}{3(n+1)} = \frac{4n+2}{3n+3}</cmath>

We are given that this ratio is exactly <imath>32\%</imath> greater than <imath>1</imath>, which means:
<cmath>\frac{4n+2}{3n+3}=\frac{132}{100} = \frac{33}{25}</cmath>

Note that the fraction <imath>\frac{33}{25}</imath> is in simplest form. For the denominator <imath>3(n+1)</imath> to reduce to a multiple of <imath>25</imath> after canceling out any common factors with the numerator, <imath>n+1</imath> must be a multiple of <imath>25</imath>.

We test the given answer choices for <imath>n+1</imath>:
* (A) <imath>19 + 1 = 20</imath> (not a multiple of 25)
* (B) <imath>39 + 1 = 40</imath> (not a multiple of 25)
* (C) <imath>49 + 1 = 50</imath> (multiple of 25)
* (D) <imath>89 + 1 = 90</imath> (not a multiple of 25)
* (E) <imath>169 + 1 = 170</imath> (not a multiple of 25)

Only <imath>n = 49</imath> satisfies.

<cmath>n = \boxed{\textbf{(C) } 49}</cmath>

==Solution 3==
Let <imath>n=2k-1.</imath> The median of the sequence would then be <imath>k^2,</imath> while the mean would be <imath>\frac{1^2+2^2+3^2+...+(2k-1)^2}{2k-1}=\frac{(2k-1)(2k)(4k-1)}{6(2k-1)}=\frac{(k)(4k-1)}{3}.</imath> 

Equating, we get <imath>\frac{(k)(4k-1)}{3}=1.32(k^2)\implies 4k-1=3.96k\implies k=25\implies n=\boxed{\text{(C) }49}</imath> ~Tacos_are_yummy_1
