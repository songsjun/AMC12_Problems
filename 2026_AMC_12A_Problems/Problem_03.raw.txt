==Problem==

Half of the internal angles of a polygon measure <imath>150^{\circ}</imath> each, and the other half of its internal angles measure <imath>190^{\circ}</imath> each. How many sides does the polygon have?

<imath>\textbf{(A)}\   36  \qquad 	\textbf{(B)}\   40  \qquad 	\textbf{(C)}\   60  \qquad 	\textbf{(D)}\   72  \qquad 	\textbf{(E)}\   180</imath>

==Solution 1==
Let the polygon have n sides. The sum of the interior angles of this polygon is <imath>(n-2)\cdot 180</imath>. <imath>\frac{n}{2}</imath> of the angles are <imath>150</imath> and <imath>\frac{n}{2}</imath> of the angles are <imath>190</imath>, so
<cmath>\frac{n}{2} \cdot 150 + \frac{n}{2} \cdot 190= (n-2)\cdot 180</cmath>
<cmath>75n + 95n = 180n - 360</cmath>
<cmath>10n = 360</cmath>
<cmath>n = 36</cmath>
Therefore, our answer is <imath>\boxed{\textbf{(A)}\hspace{1mm}36}</imath>
<imath>\newline</imath>

==Solution 2==
Because we know that half of the angles are <imath>190^c</imath> and the other half is <imath>150^c</imath>, the average angle is <imath>\frac{190^c+150^c}{2} = 170^c</imath> on average for each angle. If the average is <imath>170^c</imath>, then <cmath>\frac{180*(n-2)}{n} = 170</cmath>
<cmath>170n=180*(n-2) \to 0 = 10n-360</cmath>
<cmath>10n=360 \to n = \boxed{36}</cmath>

==Solution 4==
We can find the average exterior angle by doing <imath>180^\circ-150^\circ=30^\circ</imath> for the first angle and <imath>180^\circ-190^\circ=-10^\circ</imath> for the second. Since there are an equal amount of both exterior angles, we can find the average exterior angle:
<cmath>\frac{30^\circ+\left(-10^\circ\right)}{2} = 10^\circ</cmath>
Since the sum of the exterior angles of any polygon is <imath>360^\circ</imath>
<cmath>\frac{360^\circ}{10^\circ}=\boxed{\textbf{(A)}\hspace{1mm}36}</cmath>
