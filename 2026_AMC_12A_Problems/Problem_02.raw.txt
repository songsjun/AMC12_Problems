==Problem==
Square <imath>ABCD</imath> has side length <imath>24</imath>. Let <imath>E</imath> be the midpoint of <imath>\overline{BC}</imath>, and let <imath>F</imath> be the intersection of <imath>\overline{AE}</imath> and <imath>\overline{BD}</imath>.

<asy>
unitsize(0.12cm);

pair A = (0,24);
pair B = (0,0);
pair C = (24,0);
pair D = (24,24);
pair E = (B + C)/2;
pair F = intersectionpoint(A--E, B--D);

draw(A--B--C--D--cycle, linewidth(1));
draw(A--E, linewidth(1));
draw(B--D, linewidth(1));

dot(A);
dot(B);
dot(C);
dot(D);
dot(E);
dot(F);

label("$A$", A + 3.3*dir(135));
label("$B$", B + 3.3*dir(-135));
label("$C$", C + 3.3*dir(-45));
label("$D$", D + 3.3*dir(45));
label("$E$", E + 2.6*dir(-90));
label("$F$", F + 2.7*dir(0));
</asy>

What is the area of <imath>\triangle BEF</imath>?

<imath>
	\textbf{(A)}\   36  \qquad
	\textbf{(B)}\   40  \qquad
	\textbf{(C)}\   45  \qquad
	\textbf{(D)}\   48  \qquad
	\textbf{(E)}\   52  
</imath>

==Solution 1==
To find the intersection point of the two lines, we can turn them into a system of equations. <imath>\overline{AFE}</imath> becomes <imath>y=-2x+24</imath>, and <imath>\overline{BFD}</imath> becomes <imath>y=x</imath>. Solving, we get <imath>y=x=8</imath>. Therefore the height of the triangle is <imath>8</imath> and the base is <imath>12</imath>. So <imath>\frac{8\cdot12}{2}=\boxed{48}</imath>.
~MrThinker

==Solution 2==
<imath>\overline{AD}</imath> is parallel to <imath>\overline{BC}</imath>, so <imath>\angle{ADF} = \angle{FBE}</imath> and <imath>\angle{DAF} = \angle{FEB}</imath>. By the AA similarity theorem, <imath>ADF \sim EBF</imath>. Let the height of the triangle <imath>EBF</imath> be <imath>x</imath>. <imath>AD = 24</imath> and<imath>EB = 12</imath>, so the height of the triangle <imath>DAF</imath> is twice the height of <imath>FEB</imath>. The sum of the two heights covers the whole height of the rectangle, so <imath>3x = 24</imath>, and <imath>x = 8</imath>. Therefore, the area of the triangle is <imath>\frac{12 \cdot8}{2} = 48</imath> <imath>\fbox{D}</imath>. <imath>\newline</imath>

==Solution 3==
We can see that <imath>\overline{AE}</imath> is a median of <imath>\triangle ABC</imath>. Let <imath>\overline{BD}</imath> intersect <imath>\overline{AC}</imath> at <imath>G</imath>, so <imath>\overline{BG}</imath> is another median and <imath>F</imath> is the centroid. The medians of <imath>\triangle ABC</imath> split it into six triangles of equal area, and <imath>\triangle BEF</imath> is one of them. Therefore,
<cmath>[BEF]=\frac{1}{6}[ABC]=\frac{1}{6}\cdot288=\boxed{\textbf{(D)}~48}</cmath>

~ [[Logibyte]]

==Solution 4 (Coordinate geometry)==
We can solve this problem using coordinate geometry. Define B to be <imath>(0,0)</imath>. We can find the intersection of lines <imath>\overline{BD}</imath> and <imath>\overline{AE}</imath> by defining these lines as <imath>y=x</imath> and <imath>y=-2x+24</imath>, respectively. Solving these equations, we find the intersection point to be at <imath>(8,8)</imath> Finally, using Shoelace Theorem, we find the total area to be <imath>\frac{1}{2}|(0+0+12\cdot 8)-(0+0+0)|=48</imath>, so the answer is <imath>\boxed{\textbf{D}}</imath>
~Voidling

== Solution 6 (Area ratios) ==
We know that triangle <imath>ABE</imath> has area <imath>\frac{12 \times 24}{2}</imath>, which is just <imath>144</imath>. Since the line <imath>BD</imath> bisects the angle <imath>\angle ABE</imath>, we can use the properties of the angle bisector theorem. By the angle bisector theorem:
<cmath>\frac{AF}{FE} = \frac{AB}{BE}</cmath>
<cmath>\frac{AF}{FE} = \frac{24}{12} = \frac{2}{1}</cmath>
Therefore, <imath>AF = 2FE</imath>. This means that <imath>FE = \frac{1}{3}AE</imath>, which helps us conclude that the area of <imath>\triangle BFE</imath> is <imath>\frac{1}{3}</imath> of the area of <imath>\triangle ABE</imath>. 

So our answer is:
<cmath>\frac{144}{3} = \boxed{48}</cmath>

~Chiragnoodles


== Solution 7 (Bashing) ==
We know that square <imath>ABCD</imath> has area of 576, triangle <imath>ABE</imath> has area of 144 and triangle <imath>BCD</imath> has area 288. Now we use the answer choices to figure out the area of the remaining two triangles. Using 48 as the area of triangle <imath>BEF</imath> we can find that the triangle’s height to be 8. This means triangle <imath>AFD</imath> has a height of 24 - 8 = 16. Now we calculate the area of triangle <imath>AFD</imath> to be 192. Since triangle <imath>ABE</imath> and <imath>BCD</imath> overlap we must subtract the area of <imath>BEF</imath>. We get 144 + 288 + 192 - 48 =576. Since 576 is the area of <imath>ABCD</imath> we know the area of <imath>BEF</imath> is 48. Hence we get answer <cmath>\boxed{48}</cmath>

==Solution 9 (Cross Product)==
Establish a coordinate system with point <imath>B</imath> at the origin <imath>(0,0)</imath>, <imath>C</imath> at <imath>(24,0)</imath>, <imath>A</imath> at <imath>(0,24)</imath>, and <imath>D</imath> at <imath>(24,24)</imath>. Since <imath>E</imath> is the midpoint of <imath>\overline{BC}</imath>, its coordinates are <imath>(12,0)</imath>.

This allows us to write linear equations representing lines <imath>AE</imath> and <imath>BD</imath>:
* Line <imath>AE</imath> passes through <imath>(0,24)</imath> and <imath>(12,0)</imath>, giving <imath>y = -2x + 24</imath>.
* Line <imath>BD</imath> passes through <imath>(0,0)</imath> and <imath>(24,24)</imath>, giving <imath>y = x</imath>.

Setting these equations equal to find their intersection point <imath>F</imath>:
<cmath>x = -2x + 24 \implies 3x = 24 \implies x = 8</cmath>
Thus, the intersection <imath>F</imath> is at <imath>(8,8)</imath>. Since point <imath>B</imath> is at <imath>(0,0)</imath>, we can represent two sides of <imath>\triangle BEF</imath> as vectors in three dimensions (assuming a <imath>z</imath>-component of <imath>0</imath>):
<cmath>\overrightarrow{BF} = \langle 8, 8, 0 \rangle \quad \text{and} \quad \overrightarrow{BE} = \langle 12, 0, 0 \rangle</cmath>

Taking the cross product of these two vectors gives:
<cmath>\overrightarrow{BF} \times \overrightarrow{BE} = \begin{vmatrix} \mathbf{i} & \mathbf{j} & \mathbf{k} \\ 8 & 8 & 0 \\ 12 & 0 & 0 \end{vmatrix} = \langle 0, 0, (8)(0) - (8)(12) \rangle = \langle 0, 0, -96 \rangle</cmath>

Taking half the magnitude of the cross product gives the area of <imath>\triangle BEF</imath>:
<cmath>\text{Area} = \frac{1}{2} \left\| \overrightarrow{BF} \times \overrightarrow{BE} \right\| = \frac{1}{2} \sqrt{0^2 + 0^2 + (-96)^2} = \frac{96}{2} = \boxed{\textbf{(D) } 48}</cmath>
~thesuperioraxolotl
