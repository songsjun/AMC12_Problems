==Problem==
A sphere with center <imath>O</imath> and radius <imath>9</imath> cuts three mutually perpendicular planes that meet at point <imath>P</imath>, forming three circular cross-sections on these planes, which have radii <imath>7, 4,</imath> and <imath>3</imath>. What is <imath>OP</imath>?

<imath>\textbf{(A)}~10\qquad\textbf{(B)}~11\qquad\textbf{(C)}~12\qquad\textbf{(D)}~13\qquad\textbf{(E)}~14</imath>

// Draw the near or far part of a section circle.
void section(triple H, triple u, triple v, real r,
             pen ink, pen pale, bool near) {
  pair Q=pr(H), U=pr(r*u), V=pr(r*v);
  transform T=(Q.x,Q.y,U.x,V.x,U.y,V.y);
  real m=dep(H);
  real k=r*sqrt(dep(u)^2+dep(v)^2);
  path g;

  if(m>=k) {
    if(!near) return;
    g=T*unitcircle;
  } else if(m<=-k) {
    if(near) return;
    g=T*unitcircle;
  } else {
    real phi=atan2(dep(v),dep(u))*180/pi;
    real t=acos(-m/k)*180/pi;
    g=T*(near ? arc((0,0),1,phi-t,phi+t)
              : arc((0,0),1,phi+t,phi+360-t));
  }

  if(near) foreground(g,ink+linewidth(1.15));
  else draw(g,pale+dashed+linewidth(0.8));
}

==Solution 1==

Every point on the three cross-sections also lies on the sphere. Therefore, each of those points is <imath>9</imath> away from <imath>O</imath> from the definition of the sphere. Let the centers of the circles with radii <imath>7</imath>, <imath>4</imath>, and <imath>3</imath> be <imath>A</imath>, <imath>B</imath>, and <imath>C</imath>, respectively. By the Pythagorean Theorem, 
<cmath>OA = \sqrt{9^2 - 7^2} = \sqrt{32}.</cmath>
<cmath>OB = \sqrt{9^2 - 4^2} = \sqrt{65}.</cmath>
<cmath>OC = \sqrt{9^2 - 3^2} = \sqrt{72}.</cmath>

Assume that the planes are the <imath>xy</imath> plane, the <imath>xz</imath> plane, and the <imath>yz</imath> plane, all of which are mutually perpendicular. The distance from an arbitrary point to each plane is equal to the <imath>z</imath>, <imath>y</imath>, and <imath>z</imath> coordinates of that point. Observe that <imath>OA</imath>, <imath>OB</imath>, and <imath>OC</imath> are the distances from <imath>O</imath> to each of the planes. Then the distance between <imath>O</imath> and the origin, or point <imath>P</imath>, is equal to <imath>\sqrt{x^2+y^2+z^2}</imath>, where <imath>x</imath>, <imath>y</imath>, and <imath>z</imath> are the coordinates of <imath>O</imath>. By our reasoning above, the coordinates of <imath>O</imath> are <imath>(\sqrt{32},\sqrt{65},\sqrt{72})</imath>. Plugging in, we find that 
<cmath>OP = \sqrt{(\sqrt{32})^2+(\sqrt{65})^2+(\sqrt{72})^2} = \sqrt{32+65+72} = \sqrt{169} = \boxed{13},</cmath>
so the answer is <imath>\boxed{(D)\, 13}</imath>.
