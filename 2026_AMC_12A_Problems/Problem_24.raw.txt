==Problem 24==

Trapezoid <imath>ABCD</imath> has <imath>AB = 4</imath>, <imath>CD = 5</imath>, and <imath>\angle DAB = \angle ABC = 90^\circ</imath>. Circles <imath>\omega_1</imath> and <imath>\omega_2</imath> lie inside the trapezoid as follows: <imath>\omega_1</imath> is tangent to <imath>\overline{AB}</imath>, <imath>\overline{AD}</imath>, and <imath>\overline{DC}</imath>; <imath>\omega_2</imath> is tangent to <imath>\overline{AB}</imath>, <imath>\overline{BC}</imath>, and <imath>\overline{DC}</imath>; and <imath>\omega_1</imath> and <imath>\omega_2</imath> are externally tangent to each other. The distance between the centers of <imath>\omega_1</imath> and <imath>\omega_2</imath> is <imath>m - n\sqrt{p}</imath>, where <imath>m</imath>, <imath>n</imath>, and <imath>p</imath> are positive integers such that <imath>p</imath> is not divisible by the square of any prime. What is <imath>m + n + p</imath>?

<imath>\textbf{(A)}\quad 30 \qquad\qquad \textbf{(B)}\quad 47 \qquad\qquad \textbf{(C)}\quad 53 \qquad\qquad \textbf{(D)}\quad 62 \qquad\qquad \textbf{(E)}\quad 74</imath>

==Diagram==

==Solution 1==

Let the circles have centers <imath>O_1,O_2</imath> and radii <imath>r_1,r_2</imath>. We write <imath>d=O_1O_2</imath>. Since the circles are externally tangent,
<cmath>
d=r_1+r_2.
</cmath>

Let <imath>P,Q</imath> be the feet of the perpendiculars from <imath>O_1,O_2</imath> to <imath>AB</imath>. Tangency to <imath>AD</imath> and <imath>BC</imath> gives <imath>AP=r_1</imath> and <imath>QB=r_2</imath>. Thus
<cmath>
PQ=AB-AP-QB=4-r_1-r_2=4-d.
</cmath>

Extend <imath>AB</imath> and <imath>CD</imath> to meet at <imath>E</imath>, and let <imath>\theta=\angle DEB</imath>. Each center is equally distant from the lines <imath>AB</imath> and <imath>CD</imath>, so both centers lie on the same angle bisector. Therefore, <imath>O_1O_2</imath> makes an angle of <imath>\frac{\theta}{2}</imath> with <imath>AB</imath>.

<asy>

Since <imath>AD</imath> and <imath>BC</imath> are perpendicular to <imath>AB</imath>, the projection of <imath>CD</imath> onto <imath>AB</imath> has length <imath>4</imath>. Hence
<cmath>
\cos\theta=\frac{4}{5},
\qquad
\cos\frac{\theta}{2}
=\sqrt{\frac{1+\frac45}{2}}
=\frac3{\sqrt{10}}.
</cmath>

Projecting <imath>O_1O_2</imath> onto <imath>AB</imath> gives
<cmath>
4-d=PQ=d\cos\frac{\theta}{2}
=\frac{3d}{\sqrt{10}}.
</cmath>
Solving,
<cmath>
d=\frac{4\sqrt{10}}{\sqrt{10}+3}
=40-12\sqrt{10}.
</cmath>
Thus <imath>m+n+p=40+12+10=\boxed{62}</imath>, we choose <imath>\boxed{\textbf{(D)}}</imath>.

==Solution 2 (Constructions)==

===Solution===

Let <imath>r_1</imath> and <imath>r_2</imath> be the radii of <imath>\omega_1</imath> and <imath>\omega_2</imath>, with centers <imath>O_1</imath> and <imath>O_2</imath>. Extend <imath>BA</imath> and <imath>CD</imath> to meet at <imath>E</imath>. Since both circles are tangent to <imath>EB</imath> and <imath>EC</imath>, both centers lie on the angle bisector of <imath>\angle BEC</imath>. Let this bisector meet <imath>BC</imath> at <imath>K</imath>, and let <imath>\angle BEK = \angle KEC = \theta</imath>.

Now, drop the perpendicular from <imath>O_1</imath> to <imath>BC</imath>, and draw the line through <imath>O_2</imath> parallel to <imath>BC</imath>. Let these lines meet at <imath>F</imath>. Since the circles are externally tangent at <imath>T</imath>, and both are tangent to <imath>AB</imath>,
<cmath>O_1O_2 = r_1 + r_2, \qquad O_2F = r_2 - r_1, \qquad O_1F = 4 - r_1 - r_2.</cmath>

Let <imath>P</imath> be the projection of <imath>D</imath> onto <imath>BC</imath>. Then <imath>DP = AB = 4</imath> and <imath>DC = 5</imath>, so <imath>\triangle DPC</imath> is a <imath>3</imath>-<imath>4</imath>-<imath>5</imath> triangle with <imath>PC = 3</imath>. Since <imath>DP \parallel EB</imath>, we have <imath>\triangle EBC \sim \triangle DPC</imath>, so <imath>EB : BC : EC = 4 : 3 : 5</imath>.

By the Angle Bisector Theorem on <imath>\triangle EBC</imath>,
<cmath>\frac{BK}{KC} = \frac{EB}{EC} = \frac{4}{5}, \quad\text{so}\quad BK = \frac{4}{9}BC.</cmath>
Then <imath>\frac{BK}{EB} = \frac{4}{9}\cdot\frac{3}{4} = \frac{1}{3}</imath>, so <imath>\triangle EBK</imath> is a <imath>1 : 3 : \sqrt{10}</imath> right triangle with <imath>BK : EB : EK = 1 : 3 : \sqrt{10}</imath>.

Since <imath>O_1F \parallel EB</imath>, we have <imath>\angle FO_1O_2 = \angle BEK = \theta</imath>, so <imath>\triangle O_1FO_2 \sim \triangle EBK</imath>. Therefore
<cmath>O_2F : O_1F : O_1O_2 = 1 : 3 : \sqrt{10}.</cmath>

Let <imath>d = r_1 + r_2</imath>. Using <imath>O_1F : O_1O_2 = 3 : \sqrt{10}</imath>,
<cmath>\frac{4 - d}{d} = \frac{3}{\sqrt{10}} \implies 4\sqrt{10} = d\left(\sqrt{10} + 3\right) \implies d = \frac{4\sqrt{10}}{\sqrt{10} + 3}.</cmath>
Rationalizing the denominator,
<cmath>d = 4\sqrt{10}\left(\sqrt{10} - 3\right) = 40 - 12\sqrt{10}.</cmath>

Thus <imath>O_1O_2 = 40 - 12\sqrt{10}</imath>, so <imath>m + n + p = 40 + 12 + 10 = \boxed{\textbf{(D)}~62}</imath>.

~ Aeioujyot

==Solution 3==
Let <imath>A=(0,0)</imath>, <imath>B=(4,0)</imath>, <imath>D=(0,h)</imath>, and <imath>C=(4,h+3)</imath>.

Since <imath>AB=4</imath> and <imath>CD=5</imath>, the Pythagorean theorem gives the vertical difference between <imath>C</imath> and <imath>D</imath> as <imath>3</imath>.

The equation of line <imath>DC</imath> is
<cmath> 3x-4y+4h=0. </cmath>

Let the radii of <imath>\omega_1</imath> and <imath>\omega_2</imath> be <imath>r_1</imath> and <imath>r_2</imath>, respectively.

Since <imath>\omega_1</imath> is tangent to both <imath>AB</imath> and <imath>AD</imath>, its center is <imath>(r_1,r_1)</imath>.

Since <imath>\omega_2</imath> is tangent to both <imath>AB</imath> and <imath>BC</imath>, its center is <imath>(4-r_2,r_2)</imath>.

Using the distance formula for a point and a line, the tangency of <imath>\omega_1</imath> to <imath>DC</imath> gives

<cmath> \frac{3r_1-4r_1+4h}{5}=r_1. </cmath>

Simplifying,

<cmath> 4h-r_1=5r_1 </cmath>

<cmath> r_1=\frac{2h}{3}. </cmath>

Similarly, the tangency of <imath>\omega_2</imath> to <imath>DC</imath> gives

<cmath> \frac{3(4-r_2)-4r_2+4h}{5}=r_2. </cmath>

Simplifying,

<cmath> 12+4h=12r_2 </cmath>

<cmath> r_2=1+\frac{h}{3}. </cmath>

Since the circles are tangent to each other, the distance between their centers equals the sum of their radii. Thus,

<cmath> (4-r_1-r_2)^2+(r_2-r_1)^2 =(r_1+r_2)^2. </cmath>

Expanding and simplifying,

<cmath> (r_2-r_1)^2=8(r_1+r_2)-16. </cmath>

Substituting our expressions for the radii,

<cmath> \left(1-\frac{h}{3}\right)^2 =8(h+1)-16. </cmath>

Multiplying by <imath>9</imath> and simplifying,

<cmath> h^2-78h+81=0. </cmath>

Using the quadratic formula,

<cmath> h=\frac{78\pm\sqrt{78^2-4(81)}}{2} </cmath>

<cmath> h=39\pm12\sqrt{10}. </cmath>

Since the circles must fit inside the trapezoid, we take the smaller root:

<cmath> h=39-12\sqrt{10}. </cmath>

Finally, the distance between the centers is

<cmath> r_1+r_2=1+h. </cmath>

Therefore,

<cmath> d=40-12\sqrt{10}. </cmath>

Hence, <imath>m=40</imath>, <imath>n=12</imath>, and <imath>p=10</imath>, so

<cmath> m+n+p=40+12+10=\boxed{62}. </cmath>

The answer is <imath>\boxed{\text{(D) }62}</imath>.
