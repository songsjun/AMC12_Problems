==Problem==
A sign in a parking lot states that the total price includes a <imath>27</imath>% tax. To the nearest whole number, what percentage of the amount a customer pays is this tax?

<imath>\textbf{(A)}~21\% \qquad \textbf{(B)}~22\% \qquad \textbf{(C)}~27\% \qquad \textbf{(D)}~37\% \qquad \textbf{(E)}~73\%</imath>

==Solution 1==
Let the original ticket be <imath>\$100</imath>. Then the tax would be <imath>\$100 \cdot 27\% = \$27</imath>. The customer pays <imath>100+27=\$127</imath>, so the percentage is <imath>\frac{27}{127}=\fbox{\textbf{(A)}\ 21\%}</imath>.

~MrThinker ~minor edits by Squishy64

==Solution 2==

Let <imath>a</imath> represent the cost of the ticket. Then, the total cost (with tax) is <imath>\frac{127}{100} a</imath>. The tax is <imath>\frac{27}{100}a</imath>. Thus tax takes up <imath>\frac{27}{127}</imath>. Through long division to three decimals, this is 0.213, or 21.3%, and so the answer is <imath>\fbox{\textbf{(A)}\ 21\%}</imath>.
Note: in this problem, don't take the shortcut by estimating unless you're really good at it.
